# The order of a land-home project

A land-home project puts a manufactured home on a piece of land. The usual order is below.
Use it to ask questions in the order the answers are needed, and to tell someone what
comes next. Counties differ; the county's own permit list wins over this one.

1. **Before you buy.** Price and terms. Legal access to a road. Whether zoning and any deed
   restrictions allow a manufactured home. Flood zone, wetlands, soils for septic, slope.
   Water (public or well), sewer (public or septic), power. A survey.
2. **Plan the package.** Which home: new or used, single or multi-section, size. Where it
   sits on the lot, and where the well, septic, drain field and driveway go. The pad and
   foundation, including any foundation a loan requires. Permits. The budget, with real
   quotes for site work.
3. **Build the crew.** Site prep, septic, well, installer, electrician, and the rest of the
   roles in references/pro-roles.md. Someone has to own the schedule, often a site general
   contractor or the dealer.
4. **Bids and contracts.** Quotes for the same scope from more than one pro. Clear
   allowances, exclusions, who pulls each permit, and the payment schedule.
5. **Build to the certificate of occupancy (CO).** Permits, clearing and pad, septic and
   well, delivery and setup, utilities, skirting and steps, inspections, then the CO from
   the county building department.

Typical holdups: no legal access, zoning or deed restrictions that bar manufactured homes,
soils that fail for a standard septic system, wetlands, flood zones that raise the floor or
the insurance, the cost of bringing power, and delivery routes too tight for the home.
